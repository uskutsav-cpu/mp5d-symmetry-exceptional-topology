# Equal-spin symmetry structure and a structural no-go

Status of everything below: **symmetry derived** (exact, machine-verified in
`tests/symmetry/`), except where explicitly marked *conjectured* or *open*.
No numerical QNM frequency has been computed yet, so nothing here depends on a
solver.

## 1. Sector decomposition holds at every δ

`∂_φ` and `∂_ψ` are Killing vectors of the Myers–Perry metric for **arbitrary**
`a`, `b`. The mode ansatz `Φ ∝ e^{−iωt + i m₁φ + i m₂ψ} R(r) S(θ)` is therefore
exact everywhere in parameter space, and the separated problem never couples
different `(m₁, m₂)`.

> The full scalar operator is block diagonal with respect to `(m₁, m₂)` on the
> entire `(s, δ, μ)` parameter space, not just on the equal-spin surface.

## 2. What the enhanced symmetry does at δ = 0

At `δ = 0`:

* the spheroidicity `c² = 4sδ(ω²−μ²)` vanishes identically, so
  `Â = l(l+2)` exactly (round S³ Laplacian);
* the radial equation depends on `(m₁, m₂)` only via `m = m₁ + m₂`;
* `Ω_a = Ω_b`, so the horizon condition also depends only on `m`.

Hence the equal-spin spectrum is `ω = ω(l, m, N)` and the degenerate set at
fixed `(l, m, N)` is the `(l+1)`-dimensional SU(2) irrep

```
{ (n, m₁, m₂) : 2n + |m₁| + |m₂| = l,  m₁ + m₂ = m }.
```

## 3. The no-go

**Claim (structural).** The enhanced-`U(2)` degeneracy at `δ = 0` cannot become
defective, and cannot unfold into exceptional points, at any `δ`.

*Argument.* The `l+1` degenerate states of a fixed `(l, m, N)` multiplet carry
**distinct** `(m₁, m₂)`. By §1 they occupy blocks of the operator that are
decoupled for every `(s, δ, μ)`. A degeneracy realized as a direct sum of
one-dimensional eigenspaces in distinct blocks has geometric multiplicity equal
to its algebraic multiplicity, so it is diagonalizable, i.e. semisimple. Turning
on `δ` moves each block's eigenvalue independently but never introduces a
coupling term between them, so no Jordan chain can form. ∎

This rules out target scenario **§2.1** of the specification ("a
symmetry-protected ordinary degeneracy that splits into exceptional lines")
*for the U(2) multiplet degeneracies specifically*. It is a genuine obstruction,
not a numerical failure: the conserved azimuthal charges forbid the mixing that
an exceptional point requires.

## 4. Where exceptional structure remains possible

Everything above leaves exactly one channel open. Within a **single fixed
`(m₁, m₂)` sector**, modes are labelled by the angular branch `l` and the
overtone `N`. Two such branches can collide, and nothing forbids their
eigenvectors from coalescing. So:

> Any exceptional point in this system must occur **within one `(m₁, m₂)`
> sector, between different `(l, N)` branches.**

The role of the equal-spin symmetry is then *not* to protect the degeneracy but
to **constrain the unfolding**, in two ways:

### 4a. The `s δ` product law

`c² = 4sδ(ω²−μ²)` means the angular operator is degenerate on the *union of two
lines* `δ = 0` and `s = 0` in the `(s, δ)` plane, not just on `δ = 0`. The
angular contribution to any splitting is controlled by the product `sδ`, so
every angular-driven structure in the `(s, δ)` plane must be invariant under the
`ℤ₂ × ℤ₂` generated by `s → −s` and `δ → −δ` acting on `c²`. *Conjectured
consequence:* exceptional sets driven by angular-radial branch interaction come
in quadruples related by these reflections. **Open — needs the radial solver.**

### 4b. Exact parity in the diagonal sectors

The exchange `(a, m₁) ↔ (b, m₂)` is an exact symmetry (§6 of
`CONVENTIONS.md`) and acts as `δ → −δ` at fixed `s`. For a generic sector with
`m₁ ≠ m₂` it maps sector `(m₁, m₂)` to sector `(m₂, m₁)`, so it relates two
different problems and is only a consistency check.

But for the **diagonal sectors `m₁ = m₂`** it maps the sector to *itself*.
Therefore:

> In every `m₁ = m₂` sector, all spectral quantities are exactly **even
> functions of `δ`** at fixed `s`.

This is a strong and immediately usable constraint. Near a degeneracy in such a
sector, a two-mode effective model
`H_eff(s, δ, μ)` must have all coefficients even in `δ`, so its discriminant is
a function of `δ²`. Consequences (*conjectured, to be tested*):

* exceptional points in diagonal sectors occur in `±δ` pairs, born from
  `δ² = δ*²`;
* an exceptional point can sit **exactly on** the equal-spin surface `δ = 0`
  only if the discriminant vanishes there, which is one further real condition
  and is therefore non-generic — it requires tuning a second parameter (`s`, or
  `μ`). That is precisely a codimension count that could stabilise a **junction
  or higher-order point on the symmetry surface**, which is the specification's
  primary target (§2.2, §2.3).

## 5. Immediate consequences for the search strategy

The analysis above replaces a blind three-dimensional scan with a directed plan:

1. Search **inside single `(m₁, m₂)` sectors** only. Cross-sector degeneracies
   are exactly degenerate at `δ = 0` and provably harmless.
2. Prioritise **diagonal sectors `m₁ = m₂`** (e.g. `(0,0)`, `(1,1)`, `(2,2)`),
   where `δ`-parity is exact and where an on-surface exceptional point is a
   codimension-2 rather than codimension-3 event.
3. Look for collisions between **different `l` at fixed `(m₁, m₂)`**, tuned by
   `μ`, since the scalar mass shifts the angular tower (`Λ` enters the radial
   equation additively) relative to the radial overtone ladder.
4. Use `s → −s` together with `δ → −δ` as a second exact check.

## 6. What is still unproven

* Whether any within-sector collision actually becomes defective. (**open**)
* Whether the `sδ` reflection quadruple survives at the level of the full
  nonlinear eigenvalue problem, since the radial equation depends on `s` and `δ`
  separately, not only through `sδ`. (**open — likely false in general; the
  quadruple statement in §4a may only apply to the angular factor**)
* Everything in §4a and §4b marked *conjectured*.
