\title{Symmetry control of exceptional structure in the massive scalar
quasinormal spectrum of doubly rotating five-dimensional Myers--Perry black
holes}

\author{\TODO{authors}}
\affiliation{\TODO{affiliation}}

\begin{abstract}
We ask how the enhanced equal-spin symmetry of the asymptotically flat, doubly
rotating five-dimensional Myers--Perry black hole organizes exceptional
(defective) structure in its massive scalar quasinormal spectrum. Two results
are structural. First, because $\partial_\phi$ and $\partial_\psi$ are Killing
for arbitrary rotation parameters, the quasinormal pencil is a direct sum over
azimuthal sectors $(m_1,m_2)$, and the Jordan chains of a direct sum are the
union of the summands' chains; a coincidence of frequencies belonging to
different sectors is therefore always semisimple. This closes the equal-spin
$U(2)$ multiplet channel exactly: those degeneracies are protected and
forbidden from becoming exceptional by the same fact. Second, the discrete
symmetry group is a Klein four-group acting on parameters \emph{and} sector
labels, so diagonal sectors $m_1=m_2$ are exactly even in the spin asymmetry
$\delta$; any exceptional line there must come in mirror pairs and terminate
quadratically on the equal-spin surface rather than crossing it. We then search
the surviving within-sector channel numerically: a branch atlas of $50\,558$
continued points across seven sectors, three angular branches and four
overtones each, bounds the pairwise branch gap below by $0.51$, a
rescaling-invariant root-separation diagnostic by $0.31$, and the local
discriminant by $0.26$, with the eigenvalue condition number at the strongest
interaction finite at $8.7\times10^3$. The ten tightest candidates are
classified as avoided crossings by argument-principle, monodromy and Puiseux
tests. We find no exceptional point in the searched domain. This contrasts with
the exceptional lines established for the de Sitter members of this family,
whose mechanism is tied to the near-Nariai structure and has no $\Lambda=0$
analogue.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Massive scalar quasinormal modes of five-dimensional Myers--Perry black holes
with two rotation parameters are established \cite{HuangHuang2025}. What is not
established is how the enhanced $U(2)$ symmetry of the equal-spin surface
$a=b$ organizes \emph{exceptional} (defective) structure in that spectrum. This
paper answers that question structurally rather than by survey.

\section{Setup and conventions}
Metric, horizon structure, and the separated massive Klein--Gordon system. All
conventions are re-derived symbolically from the metric and enforced by tests;
none are transcribed. Key exact results used throughout:
\begin{align}
  c^2 &= 4 s \delta\,(\omega^2-\mu^2), \\
  \Lambda\big|_{\delta=0} &= l(l+2), \qquad l = 2n+|m_1|+|m_2| .
\end{align}
The spheroidicity vanishes identically on $\delta=0$ \emph{and} on $s=0$, so the
angular operator is the round $S^3$ Laplacian on both.

\section{The sector decomposition and the cross-sector no-go}
$\partial_\phi$ and $\partial_\psi$ are Killing for arbitrary $a,b$, so the
torus $T^2$ acts on whatever Banach space carries the quasinormal pencil by
strongly continuous bounded operators. Fourier analysis for compact group
actions --- not $L^2$ theory --- gives
\begin{equation}
  T(\omega) = \bigoplus_{(m_1,m_2)\in\mathbb{Z}^2} T_{m_1m_2}(\omega).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Direct-sum Jordan structure]
For $T=\bigoplus_k T_k$ on a direct-sum domain, the Jordan chains of $T$ at
$\omega_*$ are exactly the direct sums of chains of the summands, so the maximal
chain length of $T$ equals the maximum over $k$ of that of $T_k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
A coincidence of quasinormal frequencies belonging to \emph{distinct} azimuthal
sectors cannot be defective. The total pencil is defective at $\omega_*$ if and
only if a single sector is already defective there.
\end{corollary}

In particular the $U(2)$ multiplet degeneracies at $a=b$ --- of dimension
exactly $l+1$ at each allowed $m$, and $(l+1)^2$ per level --- are protected
\emph{and} forbidden from becoming exceptional by the same fact. This closes the
most natural target and redirects the search inside a fixed sector.

\section{The discrete symmetry group}
Exchange $E$ and parity $P$ generate a Klein four-group acting on
(parameters $\times$ sector labels). Neither acts on parameters alone, so a
parameter-space symmetry survives only in sectors that the element stabilizes:
diagonal sectors $m_1=m_2$ are exactly even in $\delta$; anti-diagonal sectors
$m_1=-m_2$ are exactly even in $s$; the sector $(0,0)$ has both.

\subsection{A termination law}
Evenness in $\delta$ makes every diagonal-sector branch analytic in
$t=\delta^2$. The discriminant $D=(\omega_+-\omega_-)^2$ therefore depends on
$\delta$ only through $t$, and the exceptional set $D=0$ is a curve in
$(s,t,\mu)$. Since $\delta=\pm\sqrt{t}$ exists only for $t\ge 0$, an exceptional
line in a diagonal sector must come in mirror pairs and \emph{terminates} on the
equal-spin surface, meeting it quadratically, rather than crossing it.

\section{Numerical method}
Solvers A--D, their validated domains, and the independence rule. The
scale-free exceptional diagnostic: $|a_1/a_2|$ from a Cauchy expansion, which is
invariant under rescaling of the spectral condition, unlike $|dF/d\omega|$.

\section{Branch atlas and collision search}
Branches are continued, never sorted: identity comes from continuation history
plus a predictor-agreement test taken from the branch's own miss history. The
atlas contains $50\,558$ points over seven independent sectors
$(0,0)$, $(\pm1,\pm1)$, $(1,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(2,1)$, $(2,2)$, with
$l\in\{l_{\min},l_{\min}+2,l_{\min}+4\}$ and $N=0,\dots,3$, over
$s\le0.42$, $|\delta|\le0.20$, $\mu\le1.8$, inner horizon $r_-\le0.2287$ and
extremality $\ge0.294$. Solver A and the recurrence-free Solver C agree to
$2.1\times10^{-9}$ on nine spanning points.

Two diagnostics are used. The pairwise branch gap is free given the atlas but
sees only computed branches; a Cauchy expansion of the spectral condition gives
$|a_1/a_2|$, which estimates the distance to \emph{any} nearby zero and is
invariant under rescaling of the spectral condition. The latter matters:
$|dF/d\omega|$ at a root can be made arbitrarily small by multiplying $F$ by a
nonvanishing analytic function, so it cannot bound anything.

\section{Result}
No exceptional point exists in the searched domain, with
\begin{align}
  \min |\omega_i-\omega_j| &= 0.5116, &
  \min |a_1/a_2| &= 0.3107, \\
  \min |D| &= 0.2618, &
  \kappa_{\max} &= 8.73\times10^{3},
\end{align}
against $\kappa=18.3$ at an ordinary control mode. An exceptional point of
order two requires the first three to vanish simultaneously and $\kappa$ to
diverge. The ten tightest candidates all show the same signature: two distinct
zeros are present (argument principle, contour-moment root finding), but there
is no coalescence, no monodromy exchange around a parameter loop, and no
square-root splitting --- i.e. avoided crossings.

The one place where the spectrum does behave dramatically is the quasiresonant
limit, where the damping falls by $8.6$ orders of magnitude to
$8.7\times10^{-10}$ as $\mathrm{Re}\,\omega\to\mu$. That is a branch point in
$\mu$, not a coalescence: the neighbouring overtone remains at
$-\mathrm{Im}\,\omega\sim0.1$ throughout.

\section{Limitations}
The minimum gap is attained on the boundary of the searched box
($s=0.42$, $\delta=-0.20$, $\mu=1.8$); a probe beyond the box shows the gap
falling to $0.47$ at $s=0.45$ but rising again at larger $\mu$, so the trend
does not extrapolate to zero, and no claim is made for $s>0.45$ or $\mu>1.8$.
Overtones $N>3$ and higher $l$ were not searched.

The quasiresonant regime is not searched for exceptional points. There
$\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega\to0$ with $\Omega=\sqrt{\omega^2-\mu^2}$, so the minimum
admissible exterior-complex-scaling angle tends to $90^\circ$ and the
recurrence-free solvers are inapplicable \emph{by formulation}; no independent
confirmation is available, so the standard for a claim cannot be met. We note
that this, and not proximity to extremality, is what defeated earlier attempts
in this parameter range: ordinary modes continue to extremality $0.0103$ with
residuals $\sim10^{-13}$. A hyperboloidal formulation, which regularizes the
outgoing behaviour geometrically rather than by rotation, is the principled
next step.

The result is a bounded numerical exclusion over a declared domain. It is not a
theorem. The symmetry statements of Secs.~III--IV are exact and independent of
the numerics; they should not be conflated with the numerical exclusion.
