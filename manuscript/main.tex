\section{Introduction}
Massive scalar quasinormal modes of five-dimensional Myers--Perry black holes
with two rotation parameters are established \cite{HuangHuang2025}. What is not
established is how the enhanced $U(2)$ symmetry of the equal-spin surface
$a=b$ organizes \emph{exceptional} (defective) structure in that spectrum. This
paper answers that question structurally rather than by survey.

\section{Setup and conventions}
Metric, horizon structure, and the separated massive Klein--Gordon system. All
conventions are re-derived symbolically from the metric and enforced by tests;
none are transcribed. Key exact results used throughout:
\begin{align}
  c^2 &= 4 s \delta\,(\omega^2-\mu^2), \\
  \Lambda\big|_{\delta=0} &= l(l+2), \qquad l = 2n+|m_1|+|m_2| .
\end{align}
The spheroidicity vanishes identically on $\delta=0$ \emph{and} on $s=0$, so the
angular operator is the round $S^3$ Laplacian on both.

\section{The sector decomposition and the cross-sector no-go}
$\partial_\phi$ and $\partial_\psi$ are Killing for arbitrary $a,b$, so the
torus $T^2$ acts on whatever Banach space carries the quasinormal pencil by
strongly continuous bounded operators. Fourier analysis for compact group
actions --- not $L^2$ theory --- gives
\begin{equation}
  T(\omega) = \bigoplus_{(m_1,m_2)\in\mathbb{Z}^2} T_{m_1m_2}(\omega).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Direct-sum Jordan structure]
For $T=\bigoplus_k T_k$ on a direct-sum domain, the Jordan chains of $T$ at
$\omega_*$ are exactly the direct sums of chains of the summands, so the maximal
chain length of $T$ equals the maximum over $k$ of that of $T_k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
A coincidence of quasinormal frequencies belonging to \emph{distinct} azimuthal
sectors cannot be defective. The total pencil is defective at $\omega_*$ if and
only if a single sector is already defective there.
\end{corollary}

In particular the $U(2)$ multiplet degeneracies at $a=b$ --- of dimension
exactly $l+1$ at each allowed $m$, and $(l+1)^2$ per level --- are protected
\emph{and} forbidden from becoming exceptional by the same fact. This closes the
most natural target and redirects the search inside a fixed sector.

\section{The discrete symmetry group}
Exchange $E$ and parity $P$ generate a Klein four-group acting on
(parameters $\times$ sector labels). Neither acts on parameters alone, so a
parameter-space symmetry survives only in sectors that the element stabilizes:
diagonal sectors $m_1=m_2$ are exactly even in $\delta$; anti-diagonal sectors
$m_1=-m_2$ are exactly even in $s$; the sector $(0,0)$ has both.

\subsection{A termination law}
Evenness in $\delta$ makes every diagonal-sector branch analytic in
$t=\delta^2$. The discriminant $D=(\omega_+-\omega_-)^2$ therefore depends on
$\delta$ only through $t$, and the exceptional set $D=0$ is a curve in
$(s,t,\mu)$. Since $\delta=\pm\sqrt{t}$ exists only for $t\ge 0$, an exceptional
line in a diagonal sector must come in mirror pairs and \emph{terminates} on the
equal-spin surface, meeting it quadratically, rather than crossing it.
