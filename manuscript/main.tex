\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask how the enhanced equal-spin symmetry of the asymptotically flat, doubly
rotating five-dimensional Myers--Perry black hole organizes exceptional
(defective) structure in its massive scalar quasinormal spectrum. Two results
are structural and exact. First, because $\partial_\phi$ and $\partial_\psi$
remain Killing for \emph{arbitrary} rotation parameters, the quasinormal
operator pencil is an orthogonal direct sum over azimuthal sectors $(m_1,m_2)$,
and the Jordan chains of a direct sum are the union of the chains of its
summands; a coincidence of frequencies belonging to distinct sectors is
therefore necessarily semisimple. This closes the equal-spin $U(2)$ multiplet
channel exactly: those degeneracies are protected from splitting \emph{and}
forbidden from becoming exceptional by one and the same fact. Second, the
residual discrete symmetry is a Klein four-group acting on the parameters
\emph{and} on the sector labels, so that diagonal sectors $m_1=m_2$ are exactly
even in the spin asymmetry $\delta=(a-b)/2$ and anti-diagonal sectors
$m_1=-m_2$ are exactly even in $s=(a+b)/2$; consequently an exceptional line in
a diagonal sector must occur in mirror pairs and terminate quadratically on the
equal-spin surface rather than crossing it. We then search the single surviving
channel --- collisions between distinct $(\ell,N)$ branches inside a fixed
sector --- using a branch atlas of $50\,558$ continued points across seven
sectors, three angular branches and four overtones each. Three
rescaling-invariant diagnostics are bounded away from zero: the pairwise branch
gap by $0.5116$, a Cauchy root-separation estimator by $0.3107$, and the local
discriminant by $0.2618$; the eigenvalue condition number at the strongest
interaction is finite at $8.73\times10^{3}$, against $18.3$ at an ordinary
control mode. The ten tightest candidates are classified as avoided crossings by
argument-principle, monodromy and Puiseux tests. We find no exceptional point in
the searched domain, and no exceptional phenomenology in the associated
ringdown: the strongest interactions split in the damping rate rather than the
oscillation frequency. The result stands in contrast to the exceptional lines
established for the de Sitter members of this family, whose mechanism relies on
the near-Nariai structure and has no $\Lambda=0$ analogue.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Exceptional points in black-hole spectroscopy}
\label{sec:intro:eps}

Quasinormal modes are the resonances of a relativistic compact object: the poles
of the retarded Green function of the perturbation operator, and the observable
content of the ringdown
\citep{Kokkotas1999D46,Berti2009D47,Konoplya2011D48,Bertietal2025D50}. Because
the operator governing them is not self-adjoint --- energy leaks through the
horizon and out to infinity --- its spectral theory admits structures with no
Hermitian counterpart. The sharpest of these is the \emph{exceptional point}
(EP): a value of the external parameters at which two or more eigenvalues
\emph{together with their eigenvectors} coalesce, so that the generator becomes
defective and cannot be diagonalized
\citep{KatoE71,Bergholtz2019E63,Ashida2020E64}.

The physical consequences are concrete. At an EP of order two the resolvent
develops a second-order pole; the quasinormal expansion ceases to be a basis;
and the time-domain response acquires a secular contribution
$t\,e^{-\ii\omega t}$ in place of a sum of pure exponentials
\citep{PanossoMacedoetal2025B30,Motohashi2024B24}. In the surrounding parameter
region the two participating modes exhibit a square-root sensitivity to
perturbations, so that a perturbation of size $\epsilon$ shifts the eigenvalues
by $\Ord(\epsilon^{1/2})$ rather than $\Ord(\epsilon)$.

Such structures are by now documented across black-hole perturbation theory.
Branching of quasinormal modes was identified in the nearly extremal Kerr
spectrum \citep{Yangetal2012B18}; eigenvalue repulsions were shown to organize
the Kerr--Newman spectrum and to connect the Kerr and Reissner--Nordstr\"om
families \citep{Diasetal2021B19,Dias2022B20,Davey2023B21}; genuine exceptional
points, together with hysteresis and ergodic behaviour of the spectral flow,
appear for massive scalars on Kerr
\citep{Cavalcante2024B22,Cavalcante2024B23,Cavalcante2025B29}; and the resonant
excitation associated with near-degenerate pairs has been analysed in detail
\citep{Oshita2025B25,Yang2025B26,Kubota2025B27,Caoetal2025B28,Wang2026B32}. Most
directly relevant to the present work, continuous \emph{exceptional lines} have
been established for massive scalars on Kerr--de Sitter and
Myers--Perry--de Sitter, where the near-Nariai limit reduces the radial problem
to P\"oschl--Teller form and permits a semi-analytic treatment
\citep{Nakamoto2026A17,Daveyetal2022A13,Ponglertsakul2020A12}.

The non-Hermitian literature supplies the organizing framework. Exceptional sets
are generically of codimension two in a real parameter space, so that a
one-parameter family meets them only accidentally while a two-parameter family
meets them on isolated points and a three-parameter family on curves
\citep{Bergholtz2019E63}. Symmetry modifies this counting: parity--time or
generalized chiral symmetry can reduce the codimension of \emph{higher-order}
exceptional points, making third-order points generic in two parameters rather
than the four required without symmetry \citep{Mandal2021E65}, and symmetry can
protect entire exceptional chains in lattice models \citep{Zhangetal2022E66}.

\subsection{The five-dimensional Myers--Perry background}
\label{sec:intro:mp}

The five-dimensional Myers--Perry black hole \citep{Myers1986A01,Emparan2008A02}
is an unusually favourable setting in which to ask whether that machinery has a
gravitational realization with \emph{two} independent rotation parameters. Three
features make it so.

First, the massive Klein--Gordon equation separates exactly for arbitrary $a$
and $b$ \citep{Murata2007A03,Wu2008A04}, a property that extends to the Dirac
and massive vector fields \citep{Wu2008A04,Frolovetal2018A09}, so that the
spectral problem reduces to a coupled pair of ordinary differential equations
rather than a genuinely two-dimensional eigenvalue problem.

Second, the massive scalar spectrum with two arbitrary spins has been computed
\citep{Huang2025A16,Lietal2023A15,Lu2023A14}, including the long-lived branches
that appear when the scalar mass becomes comparable to the inverse horizon
scale, so that a numerical baseline exists against which a new calculation can
be validated.

Third, and decisively for the present question, the isometry group is enhanced
when the two angular momenta coincide,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:enhancement}
  \mathbb{R}\times U(1)\times U(1)\ \longrightarrow\
  \mathbb{R}\times SU(2)\times U(1),
\end{equation}
and the metric becomes cohomogeneity-one \citep{Murata2008A06}. The enhancement
\eqref{eq:enhancement} produces exact degeneracies in the perturbation spectrum.
The equal-spin surface is thus a symmetric locus embedded in a
three-dimensional parameter space $(s,\delta,\mu)$ built from the mean rotation,
the spin asymmetry and the scalar mass --- precisely the configuration in which
one expects symmetry-protected exceptional structure to appear.

\subsection{The question, and a summary of what follows}
\label{sec:intro:summary}

The natural expectation, and the standard mechanism in non-Hermitian systems
\citep{Zhangetal2022E66,Mandal2021E65}, is that the degeneracies created by
\eqref{eq:enhancement} should unfold into exceptional points once the symmetry
is broken by $\delta\ne0$.

\emph{We show that this expectation fails, and that it fails for an exact
reason rather than a numerical one.} Both azimuthal Killing vectors survive for
arbitrary $a,b$; consequently the quasinormal pencil decomposes as a direct sum
over azimuthal sectors, and the Jordan chains of a direct sum are the union of
the chains of its summands. A coincidence of frequencies drawn from distinct
sectors is therefore always semisimple. The degeneracies produced by the
enhanced symmetry are protected from splitting \emph{and} forbidden from
becoming defective by one and the same fact. This is not a statement about the
thoroughness of a search: it holds at every point of the parameter space.

The closure redirects the question rather than terminating it, and the remainder
of the paper follows that redirection.
Section~\ref{sec:setup} establishes the background geometry, the separated
system, and the singular structure of the radial equation.
Section~\ref{sec:nogo} proves the decomposition and the no-go.
Section~\ref{sec:group} classifies the residual discrete symmetry and derives
from it a termination law for exceptional lines.
Section~\ref{sec:spectral} develops the local spectral theory --- discriminant,
Puiseux expansion, monodromy --- and shows, in particular, that the derivative
of a spectral condition is not an invariant and cannot bound the distance to an
exceptional point. Section~\ref{sec:numerics} describes the numerical
formulations. Sections~\ref{sec:atlas} and~\ref{sec:results} present the branch
atlas and the resulting bounded exclusion. Section~\ref{sec:quasiresonant}
analyses the quasiresonant limit, where a solver-domain question turns out to be
physics. Sections~\ref{sec:limits} and~\ref{sec:discussion} state the
limitations and the outlook.

\section{The background and the separated problem}\label{sec:setup}
%=========================================================================

\subsection{Geometry and horizon structure}\label{sec:setup:geom}

We work in Boyer--Lindquist-like coordinates $(t,r,\theta,\phi,\psi)$ with
$\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$ and $\phi,\psi\in[0,2\pi)$, and signature $(-++++)$. The
Myers--Perry metric with two rotation parameters $a,b$ reads
\begin{align}\label{eq:metric}
  \dd s^2 =\ & -\dd t^2
    + \frac{M}{\Sigma}\Big(\dd t - a\sin^2\!\theta\,\dd\phi
      - b\cos^2\!\theta\,\dd\psi\Big)^{\!2}
    + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\,\dd r^2 + \Sigma\,\dd\theta^2 \nonumber\\
    &+ (r^2+a^2)\sin^2\!\theta\,\dd\phi^2
     + (r^2+b^2)\cos^2\!\theta\,\dd\psi^2 ,
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmapi}
  \Sigma = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\!\theta + b^2\sin^2\!\theta ,\qquad
  \Pi = (r^2+a^2)(r^2+b^2) ,\qquad
  \Delta = \frac{\Pi - M r^2}{r^2}.
\end{equation}
Here $M$ is the Myers--Perry mass parameter and carries dimension
$(\text{length})^2$; it is \emph{not} the scalar mass, which we denote $\mu$
throughout. We set $M=1$, which fixes the length unit. The determinant
satisfies $\sqrt{|g|}=r\,\Sigma\sin\theta\cos\theta$ exactly, a relation we use
as a consistency check on the symbolic derivation.

Writing $z=r^2$, the horizon polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizonpoly}
  P(z) = (z+a^2)(z+b^2) - Mz = (z-z_+)(z-z_-) ,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zpm}
  z_\pm = \tfrac12\Big[(M-a^2-b^2)\pm\sqrt{(M-a^2-b^2)^2-4a^2b^2}\Big],
  \qquad z_+z_- = a^2b^2, \qquad z_++z_- = M-a^2-b^2 .
\end{equation}
A horizon exists if and only if $M\ge(|a|+|b|)^2$, with equality at
extremality; we use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremality}
  \mathcal{E} \equiv M - (|a|+|b|)^2
\end{equation}
as the extremality measure. The horizon angular velocities and surface gravity
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizondata}
  \Omega_a = \frac{a}{z_++a^2},\qquad
  \Omega_b = \frac{b}{z_++b^2},\qquad
  \kappa = \frac{r_+\,(z_+-z_-)}{(z_++a^2)(z_++b^2)},\qquad
  T_H = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} .
\end{equation}
At $a=b=0$ these reduce to $\Omega_a=\Omega_b=0$ and
$T_H = (D-3)/(4\pi r_+) = 1/(2\pi r_+)$ in $D=5$, as required.

Throughout we use the adapted rotation coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sdelta}
  s=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad \delta=\frac{a-b}{2},
\end{equation}
so that $\delta=0$ is the equal-spin surface on which \eqref{eq:enhancement}
holds. We quote the inner horizon as the \emph{radius} $r_-$, with
$z_-=r_-^2$; the distinction matters quantitatively, since a domain restriction
imposed on one is not the same restriction on the other.

\subsection{Separation of the massive Klein--Gordon equation}
\label{sec:setup:sep}

With the mode convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modeansatz}
  \Phi = e^{-\ii\omega t + \ii m_1\phi + \ii m_2\psi}\,R(r)\,S(\theta),
  \qquad m_1,m_2\in\mathbb{Z},
\end{equation}
the equation $(\Box-\mu^2)\Phi=0$ separates exactly. Because
$\Phi\propto e^{-\ii\omega t}$, a damped mode has $\Imm\,\omega<0$. The angular
equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular}
  \frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}
  \left[\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\frac{\dd S}{\dd\theta}\right]
  + \left[\Lambda + (\omega^2-\mu^2)\big(a^2\cos^2\!\theta+b^2\sin^2\!\theta\big)
    - \frac{m_1^2}{\sin^2\!\theta} - \frac{m_2^2}{\cos^2\!\theta}\right]S = 0 ,
\end{equation}
and the radial equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
  \frac{1}{r}\frac{\dd}{\dd r}\!\left[r\Delta\frac{\dd R}{\dd r}\right]
  + \left[\frac{W^2}{r^4\Delta} - \frac{G^2}{r^2}
    - (a^2+b^2)\omega^2 + 2\omega(am_1+bm_2) - \mu^2r^2 - \Lambda\right]R = 0 ,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WG}
  W(r) = \Pi\,\omega - m_1 a (r^2+b^2) - m_2 b (r^2+a^2) ,
  \qquad
  G = ab\,\omega - a m_2 - b m_1 .
\end{equation}
The separation constant $\Lambda$ is normalized by requiring
$\Lambda=\ell(\ell+2)$ at $a=b=0$. We re-derived \eqref{eq:angular} and
\eqref{eq:radial} symbolically from \eqref{eq:metric} in exact rational
arithmetic rather than transcribing them, and the derivation is re-executed as
an automated test on every run; the details are collected in
Appendix~\ref{app:sep}.

\subsection{The angular problem and the two degenerate surfaces}
\label{sec:setup:angular}

Substituting $u=\cos^2\!\theta$ in \eqref{eq:angular} and separating the
constant part of the potential motivates the shifted eigenvalue and the
spheroidicity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ahatc2}
  \hat A \equiv \Lambda + (\omega^2-\mu^2)b^2 ,
  \qquad
  c^2 \equiv (\omega^2-\mu^2)(a^2-b^2) .
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:angular} is then a spheroidal problem of the type studied in
\citep{Berti2005A05}. Because $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)=4s\delta$, one has the exact
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2exact}
  \boxed{\;c^2 = 4\,s\,\delta\,(\omega^2-\mu^2)\;}
\end{equation}
which is not a perturbative statement. Three consequences follow immediately.

\begin{enumerate}
\item On the equal-spin surface $\delta=0$ the spheroidicity vanishes
identically, for \emph{every} $s$, $\mu$ and $\omega$. The angular operator is
then the round Laplacian on $S^3$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambdaround}
  \hat A = \ell(\ell+2), \qquad \ell = 2n+|m_1|+|m_2|,\quad n\ge0,
\end{equation}
with the full $(\ell+1)^2$-fold $SO(4)$ degeneracy.

\item The same holds on $s=0$, i.e.\ for counter-rotating $a=-b$. This is a
second exactly degenerate surface in the $(s,\delta)$ plane, and it is not the
one associated with \eqref{eq:enhancement}.

\item The angular degeneracy is lifted only by the \emph{product} $s\delta$, so
the two surfaces intersect transversally at the origin of the $(s,\delta)$
plane.
\end{enumerate}

Moreover, at $\delta=0$ the radial equation \eqref{eq:radial} and the horizon
condition depend on $(m_1,m_2)$ only through the combination
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mtotal}
  m = m_1+m_2 .
\end{equation}
This is the algebraic origin of the multiplet structure analysed in
Section~\ref{sec:nogo:multiplets}.

\subsection{Singular structure of the radial equation}
\label{sec:setup:singular}

In the variable $z=r^2$ the radial equation \eqref{eq:radial} has apparent
singularities at $z=0$, $z=z_\pm$, and an irregular singular point at infinity.
The point $z=0$ is only apparent: using $W(0)=abG$ and $P(0)=a^2b^2$ one finds
that the would-be $1/z$ terms cancel identically whenever $ab\ne0$, so that
$z=0$ is an \emph{ordinary} point of \eqref{eq:radial}. The equation is
therefore of confluent Heun type, with exactly two regular singular points
$z_\pm$ and one irregular point at infinity.

At the outer horizon the indicial equation gives the exponents $\pm\ii\sigma_+$
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmaplus}
  \sigma_+ = \frac{\omega - m_1\Omega_a - m_2\Omega_b}{2\kappa},
\end{equation}
and the quasinormal boundary condition selects the ingoing branch
$(z-z_+)^{-\ii\sigma_+}$. At infinity, writing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omegadef}
  \Omega \equiv \sqrt{\omega^2-\mu^2},
\end{equation}
the outgoing solution behaves as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinityBC}
  R(r)\ \sim\ e^{\ii\Omega r}\,r^{-3/2}, \qquad r\to\infty .
\end{equation}
The exponent $-3/2$ is exact and there is \emph{no} Coulomb phase: since $D-3=2$
the radial potential in \eqref{eq:radial} is an even function of $r$ and
possesses no $1/r$ tail. This is a structural difference from the
four-dimensional massive Kerr problem, where a logarithmic phase
$\propto\log r$ is present \citep{DetweilerD53,Dolan2007D55}, and it simplifies
both the asymptotic matching and the continued-fraction construction of
Section~\ref{sec:numerics}. The Frobenius analysis behind \eqref{eq:sigmaplus}
and \eqref{eq:infinityBC} is given in Appendix~\ref{app:frobenius}.

\subsection{The quasinormal eigenvalue problem}\label{sec:setup:pencil}

Equations \eqref{eq:angular}--\eqref{eq:radial} with the boundary conditions of
Section~\ref{sec:setup:singular} define a \emph{nonlinear} eigenvalue problem:
$\omega$ enters the coefficients, and the angular eigenvalue $\Lambda$ is itself
a function of $\omega$. We write the problem abstractly as an operator pencil
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pencil}
  T(\omega)\,u = 0 ,
\end{equation}
holomorphic in $\omega$ on the relevant domain, and record the coupled scalar
form used numerically:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fdef}
  F\big(\omega;\, s,\delta,\mu,\, m_1,m_2,\ell\big) = 0 ,
  \qquad \Lambda = \Lambda(\omega;\dots),
\end{equation}
in which $\Lambda$ is recomputed at every evaluation of $F$ and never frozen.
Freezing $\Lambda$ while differentiating with respect to $\omega$ discards the
term $\partial_\Lambda F\cdot \dd\Lambda/\dd\omega$ and is a standard route to
spurious exceptional points; the derivatives used in this work are total.

\subsection{The torus action and the isotypic decomposition}
\label{sec:nogo:torus}

No component of \eqref{eq:metric} depends on $\phi$ or $\psi$. Hence
$\partial_\phi$ and $\partial_\psi$ are Killing vectors for \emph{arbitrary}
$a,b$ --- not merely on the equal-spin surface --- and the torus
$T^2=S^1_\phi\times S^1_\psi$ acts on the space carrying the pencil
\eqref{eq:pencil} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:torusaction}
  (\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}u)(t,r,\theta,\phi,\psi)
  = u(t,r,\theta,\phi-\alpha,\psi-\beta).
\end{equation}

A natural objection is that quasinormal eigenfunctions are not square
integrable --- by \eqref{eq:infinityBC} they diverge at infinity whenever
$\Imm\,\omega<0$ --- so a Fourier decomposition justified in $L^2(T^2)$ need not
be available. The objection is met by dispensing with $L^2$ altogether.

\begin{proposition}[Sector decomposition]\label{prop:decomp}
Let $X$ be any Banach space on which the pencil $T(\omega)$ of
\eqref{eq:pencil} is defined --- weighted, hyperboloidal, or a space of analytic
functions with prescribed boundary behaviour. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:directsum}
  T(\omega) \;=\; \bigoplus_{(m_1,m_2)\in\mathbb{Z}^2} T_{m_1m_2}(\omega)
\end{equation}
on a domain which is itself a direct sum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The action \eqref{eq:torusaction} is by bounded invertible operators, and is
strongly continuous, because it relabels two \emph{compact} coordinates and
touches neither $r$ nor the boundary conditions \eqref{eq:sigmaplus},
\eqref{eq:infinityBC} that define $X$. For a compact abelian group acting
strongly continuously by bounded operators on a Banach space, the isotypic
projections
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projectors}
  \Pi_{m_1m_2} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}
  e^{-\ii(m_1\alpha+m_2\beta)}\,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}
  \,\dd\alpha\,\dd\beta
\end{equation}
are bounded, mutually orthogonal idempotents summing to the identity in the
strong topology; this is Fourier analysis for compact group representations and
requires no inner product. Since the metric is $\phi,\psi$-independent, the wave
operator commutes with the generators and hence with every $\Pi_{m_1m_2}$. The
horizon exponent \eqref{eq:sigmaplus} depends only on the sector's own labels,
and the condition \eqref{eq:infinityBC} does not involve them at all, so the
domain decomposes as well. Equation \eqref{eq:directsum} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The compact directions carry the entire argument; the radial functional-analytic
setting never enters. It is worth emphasising that \eqref{eq:directsum} is a
decomposition over $(m_1,m_2)$ \emph{only}. It does not use separation of
variables in $\theta$, a point that becomes essential in
Section~\ref{sec:nogo:survives}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Jordan structure of a direct sum}\label{sec:nogo:jordan}

\begin{definition}[Jordan chain of a pencil]
A tuple $(v_0,\dots,v_{q-1})$ with $v_0\ne0$ is a Jordan chain of length $q$ for
the holomorphic pencil $T$ at $\omega_*$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jordanchain}
  \sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{1}{i!}\,T^{(i)}(\omega_*)\,v_{j-i} = 0,
  \qquad j=0,1,\dots,q-1 .
\end{equation}
The eigenvalue $\omega_*$ is \emph{semisimple} if every chain has length one,
and \emph{defective} otherwise; an exceptional point of order $q$ is a parameter
value at which the maximal chain length is $q\ge2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Jordan structure of a direct sum]\label{lem:directsum}
Let $T(\omega)=\bigoplus_k T_k(\omega)$ on a direct-sum domain, with bounded
projectors $\Pi_k$ commuting with $T$. Then the Jordan chains of $T$ at
$\omega_*$ are exactly the direct sums of Jordan chains of the summands, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxchain}
  \max\{\text{chain length of }T\text{ at }\omega_*\}
  = \max_k\ \max\{\text{chain length of }T_k\text{ at }\omega_*\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(v_0,\dots,v_{q-1})$ satisfy \eqref{eq:jordanchain}. Since $\Pi_k$ is
bounded and commutes with $T$ and hence with each $T^{(i)}$, applying $\Pi_k$ to
the $j$-th equation gives
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{1}{i!}\,T_k^{(i)}(\omega_*)\,\Pi_k v_{j-i} = 0 ,
\end{equation}
so the componentwise projections form a chain in block $k$ whenever
$\Pi_k v_0\ne0$. Conversely a chain in a single block, extended by zero on the
complement, satisfies \eqref{eq:jordanchain} for $T$. Hence every chain of $T$
is a superposition of chains of the summands, and its length cannot exceed the
longest chain occurring in any one block; \eqref{eq:maxchain} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-sector no-go]\label{thm:nogo}
Consider the quasinormal pencil of the doubly rotating five-dimensional
Myers--Perry black hole. A coincidence of quasinormal frequencies belonging to
\emph{distinct} azimuthal sectors $(m_1,m_2)\ne(m_1',m_2')$ cannot be defective.
Equivalently, $T(\omega_*)$ is defective if and only if some single sector
$T_{m_1m_2}(\omega_*)$ is already defective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} and Lemma~\ref{lem:directsum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} carries no hypothesis on the multiplicity within any
individual block, which is why it is formulated at the level of Jordan structure
rather than as an assertion about simple eigenvalues. The underlying algebra is
standard \citep{KatoE71}; what is specific to the present system is that
\eqref{eq:directsum} holds for \emph{all} $a,b$, so the conclusion is not
confined to the symmetric locus.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The equal-spin multiplets}\label{sec:nogo:multiplets}

We now identify precisely which degeneracies Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} disposes of.

\begin{proposition}[Multiplet dimension]\label{prop:multiplet}
Fix $\ell\ge0$ and $m$ with $|m|\le\ell$ and $\ell\equiv m \pmod 2$. On the
equal-spin surface the set of azimuthal labels degenerate with one another at
fixed $(\ell,m)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multipletset}
  \mathcal{M}_{\ell,m} = \big\{(m_1,m_2)\in\mathbb{Z}^2 :\ m_1+m_2=m,\
  |m_1|+|m_2|\le\ell,\ \ell-|m_1|-|m_2|\in2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}\big\},
\end{equation}
and $|\mathcal{M}_{\ell,m}| = \ell+1$, independently of $m$. Summing over the
$\ell+1$ admissible values of $m$ gives $(\ell+1)^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $m\ge0$; the case $m<0$ follows from the parity generator of
Section~\ref{sec:group}. Put $m_1=m-m_2$. For $m_2\in[0,m]$ --- that is, for
$m+1$ pairs --- one has $|m_1|+|m_2|=m$. For each $j\ge1$ the value
$|m_1|+|m_2|=m+2j$ is attained by exactly two pairs, $m_2=m+j$ and $m_2=-j$. The
constraint in \eqref{eq:multipletset} admits precisely $j\le(\ell-m)/2$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
  |\mathcal{M}_{\ell,m}| = (m+1) + 2\cdot\frac{\ell-m}{2} = \ell+1 ,
\end{equation}
independent of $m$. The admissible $m$ are $m=-\ell,-\ell+2,\dots,\ell$, of
which there are $\ell+1$, so the level total is $(\ell+1)^2$ --- the dimension
of the $SO(4)$ scalar harmonic level, as required by
\eqref{eq:Lambdaround}.
\end{proof}

By \eqref{eq:mtotal} the members of $\mathcal{M}_{\ell,m}$ share the same radial
problem at $\delta=0$ and are therefore exactly degenerate. They are also, by
construction, labelled by \emph{distinct} $(m_1,m_2)$, hence occupy decoupled
blocks of \eqref{eq:directsum}. Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} then forbids that
degeneracy from being defective at any $\delta$: the protection mechanism and
the obstruction are one and the same. Figure~\ref{fig:multiplet}(a) exhibits the
degeneracy and its lifting for the pair of sectors $(1,1)$ and $(2,0)$, which
share $m=2$.

\subsection{What survives}\label{sec:nogo:survives}

Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} eliminates the cross-sector channel entirely, and with it
the most natural route to exceptional structure in this background. The search
must therefore move \emph{inside} a fixed sector.

Within one sector, distinct angular branches $\ell$ are \emph{not} decoupled
blocks. The angular eigenvalue $\Lambda_j(\omega)$ depends on $\omega$, so the
branches labelled by $\ell$ are branches of a single analytic family rather than
summands of a direct sum, and nothing in Lemma~\ref{lem:directsum} constrains
them. This is precisely why Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} was established
without invoking separation of variables: had the decomposition been taken over
$(m_1,m_2,\ell)$, the theorem would have proved too much and would have been
false. Collisions between distinct $(\ell,N)$ branches inside a fixed sector are
the surviving target, and the remainder of the paper addresses them.

\section{The residual discrete symmetry}\label{sec:group}
%=========================================================================

\subsection{Generators}\label{sec:group:gens}

Two discrete transformations act on the pair (parameters, sector labels):
\begin{align}
  E:\ &(a,b,m_1,m_2)\ \longmapsto\ (b,a,m_2,m_1), \label{eq:Egen}\\
  P:\ &(a,b,m_1,m_2)\ \longmapsto\ (-a,-b,-m_1,-m_2). \label{eq:Pgen}
\end{align}
$E$ interchanges the labels of the two rotation planes and is an exact symmetry
of \eqref{eq:metric} together with \eqref{eq:angular}--\eqref{eq:WG}; $P$ is the
orientation reversal $\phi\to-\phi$, $\psi\to-\psi$ composed with reversal of
both rotation senses. Both are exact for arbitrary $a,b,\mu$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:klein}
  G = \{1,E,P,EP\}\ \cong\ \mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2 .
\end{equation}
In the adapted coordinates \eqref{eq:sdelta},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:groupaction}
  E:(s,\delta)\mapsto(s,-\delta),\qquad
  P:(s,\delta)\mapsto(-s,-\delta),\qquad
  EP:(s,\delta)\mapsto(-s,\delta).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stabilizers and induced parameter symmetries}
\label{sec:group:stab}

The essential observation is that \emph{no element of $G$ acts on the parameters
alone}: each also moves the sector. Consequently a symmetry of the parameter
space \emph{at fixed sector} exists only in those sectors that the corresponding
group element stabilizes. The classification is immediate from
\eqref{eq:Egen}--\eqref{eq:groupaction} and is collected in
Table~\ref{tab:stabilizers}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
sector & stabilizing element & induced symmetry at fixed sector \\
\midrule
$m_1=m_2$ (diagonal) & $E$ & $\omega(s,\delta)=\omega(s,-\delta)$ \\
$m_1=-m_2$ (anti-diagonal) & $EP$ & $\omega(s,\delta)=\omega(-s,\delta)$ \\
$(m_1,m_2)=(0,0)$ & all of $G$ & even in $\delta$ \emph{and} in $s$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Stabilizers and the parameter-space symmetries they induce. All three

$\delta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:multiplet}(b)).}
\label{tab:stabilizers}
\end{table}

The distinction is easy to over-state in the opposite direction. Reflections of
the parameter space are \emph{not} symmetries of a generic sector, and only the
three stabilized families in Table~\ref{tab:stabilizers} inherit one. In
particular, the frequently convenient assertion that exceptional sets come in
quadruples under independent reflections $s\to-s$ and $\delta\to-\delta$ holds
only in the sector $(0,0)$.

\subsection{Codimension and a termination law}\label{sec:group:termination}

Consider a diagonal sector, $m_1=m_2$. By Table~\ref{tab:stabilizers} every
branch is exactly even in $\delta$, hence --- granted analyticity in the
parameters --- an analytic function of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tdef}
  t \equiv \delta^2 .
\end{equation}
Let $\omega_\pm$ denote two interacting branches and define the discriminant of
the local two-branch problem,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ddef}
  D \equiv (\omega_+-\omega_-)^2 .
\end{equation}
Then $D$ depends on $\delta$ only through $t$, and an exceptional point of order
two is the condition $D=0$, i.e.\ two real equations --- the familiar
codimension two \citep{Bergholtz2019E63}. In the three real variables
$(s,t,\mu)$ this defines a one-dimensional curve. The physical parameter space,
however, is the double cover $\delta=\pm\sqrt{t}$, which exists only for
$t\ge0$. Two consequences follow \emph{without} knowing whether an exceptional
point exists at all.

\begin{proposition}[Mirror pairing and termination]\label{prop:termination}
In a diagonal sector, and under the stated analyticity, any exceptional line
\begin{enumerate}
\item occurs in mirror pairs, exchanged by $E$; and
\item where the curve $D=0$ in $(s,t,\mu)$ crosses $t=0$ transversally into
$t<0$, the real locus in $\delta$ terminates. Parametrizing the curve by
arclength $\tau$ with $t\approx c\tau$, $c\neq0$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrtlaw}
  \delta = \pm\sqrt{c\,\tau} ,
\end{equation}
so the two mirror branches meet at $\delta=0$ and stop, approaching the
equal-spin surface quadratically rather than crossing it as a single smooth
line.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

This is the mechanism by which the enhanced equal-spin symmetry organizes
exceptional topology in the present system: not by protecting a degeneracy ---
Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} closes that channel --- but by constraining the
\emph{unfolding}. It is the gravitational analogue of the symmetry-constrained
exceptional structures catalogued in non-Hermitian lattice models
\citep{Zhangetal2022E66,Mandal2021E65}, with the important difference that there
symmetry lowers the codimension of higher-order points, whereas here it acts on
the reality structure of the unfolding parameter. Proposition~\ref{prop:termination}
is falsifiable and was recorded before the numerical search; it remains untested,
because no exceptional line was found to exhibit it.

\section{Local spectral theory and invariant diagnostics}\label{sec:spectral}
%=========================================================================

\subsection{Normal form, Puiseux expansion and monodromy}
\label{sec:spectral:normalform}

Two interacting branches are described locally by a $2\times2$ non-Hermitian
generator $H(\bm p)$ depending on parameters $\bm p$. Its individual entries are
not observable, since any similarity transformation alters them, but the two
symmetric functions are:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TD}
  T = \omega_+ + \omega_- = \operatorname{tr}H ,
  \qquad
  D = (\omega_+-\omega_-)^2 = (\operatorname{tr}H)^2 - 4\det H .
\end{equation}
The eigenvalues are $\omega_\pm = \tfrac12\big(T\pm\sqrt D\big)$, so an EP2 is
exactly $D=0$. Near a simple zero of $D$ along a transversal direction $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:puiseux}
  \omega_\pm(t) = \omega_* + c_1 t \pm c_{1/2}\,t^{1/2}
  + c_{3/2}\,t^{3/2} + \Ord(t^{2}),
  \qquad c_{1/2}\neq0 ,
\end{equation}
which is the Puiseux expansion characteristic of a square-root branch point
\citep{KatoE71,Nennig2019E67}. Three observable signatures follow, and we use all
three:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the splitting obeys $|\omega_+-\omega_-|\sim 2|c_{1/2}|\,t^{1/2}$, in
contrast to $\Ord(t)$ at an ordinary crossing and to a nonzero constant at an
avoided crossing;
\item transporting the pair once around a small loop encircling the EP
interchanges the two branches, and two circuits restore them, so the monodromy
is the transposition;
\item a contour integral of $\dd\log$ of the spectral condition counts two zeros
inside a contour that shrinks with the candidate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why \texorpdfstring{$|\dd F/\dd\omega|$}{|dF/domega|} is not an
invariant}\label{sec:spectral:noninvariance}

It is tempting to detect a repeated root of \eqref{eq:Fdef} by monitoring
$|\dd F/\dd\omega|$ at a converged root, on the grounds that this derivative
vanishes at a double root. The diagnostic is not admissible, and the reason is
structural rather than numerical.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:noninvariance}
The spectral condition $F$ of \eqref{eq:Fdef} is determined only up to
multiplication by a nonvanishing holomorphic function. Consequently
$|\dd F/\dd\omega|$ evaluated at a root carries no invariant meaning, and no
bound derived from it bounds the distance to an exceptional point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $g$ be holomorphic and nonvanishing on a neighbourhood of the root
$\omega_1$, and set $\tilde F = gF$. Then $\tilde F$ and $F$ have identical zero
sets, and at a simple root
$\tilde F'(\omega_1) = g(\omega_1)F'(\omega_1)$, which may be given any nonzero
value by choice of $g(\omega_1)$. Leaver's continued fraction is defined only up
to precisely such a factor: it may be rescaled, or inverted at a different
depth, without altering its zeros.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}(a) exhibits the failure directly: two spectral
conditions with identical zero sets whose $|\dd F/\dd\omega|$ differ by more
than five orders of magnitude, while the invariant estimator constructed next
agrees to $2\%$.

\subsection{A rescaling-invariant separation estimator}
\label{sec:spectral:estimator}

Let $F$ be holomorphic near a simple root $\omega_1$ and write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ffactored}
  F(\omega) = c\,(\omega-\omega_1)(\omega-\omega_2)\,g(\omega),
\end{equation}
with $g$ holomorphic and nonvanishing. Expanding about $\omega_1$,
$F(\omega)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_k(\omega-\omega_1)^k$, one finds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a1a2}
  a_1 = c\,(\omega_1-\omega_2)\,g(\omega_1),
  \qquad
  a_2 = c\,g(\omega_1) + c\,(\omega_1-\omega_2)\,g'(\omega_1),
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep}
  \boxed{\;
  \left|\frac{a_1}{a_2}\right|
  = |\omega_1-\omega_2|\,\Big(1+\Ord(\omega_1-\omega_2)\Big)\;}
\end{equation}
Both the constant $c$ and the analytic factor $g$ cancel. The estimator
\eqref{eq:sep} is therefore invariant under the rescalings of
Proposition~\ref{prop:noninvariance}, vanishes exactly at an EP2, and --- unlike
a pairwise gap between \emph{tracked} branches --- responds to a partner root
that was never computed. This last property is what makes it capable of
supporting a negative result.

\subsection{Cauchy coefficients and the argument principle}
\label{sec:spectral:cauchy}

The coefficients $a_k$ are obtained from samples of $F$ on a circle rather than
by finite differencing. With $\omega = \omega_1 + \rho\,e^{\ii\vartheta}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchycoef}
  a_k = \frac{1}{2\pi\ii}\oint\frac{F(\omega)}{(\omega-\omega_1)^{k+1}}\,
  \dd\omega
  \;\simeq\; \frac{1}{N\rho^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
  F\big(\omega_1+\rho e^{\ii\vartheta_j}\big)\,e^{-\ii k\vartheta_j},
  \qquad \vartheta_j = \frac{2\pi j}{N},
\end{equation}
the trapezoidal rule being spectrally accurate for periodic analytic
integrands. The same samples yield the argument-principle count
\begin{equation}\label{eq:argprinciple}
  \mathcal{N}-\mathcal{P} = \frac{1}{2\pi\ii}\oint\frac{F'}{F}\,\dd\omega
  = \frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\arg F\Big]_{\partial\mathcal{C}} ,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{N}$ and $\mathcal{P}$ the numbers of zeros and poles inside
$\mathcal{C}$. Individual roots inside $\mathcal{C}$ are recovered from the
contour moments $\oint \omega^k F'/F\,\dd\omega$ by Newton's identities
\citep{KravanjaVanBarel2000}, a procedure that requires no starting guess and is
therefore evidence independent of any Newton iteration.

\section{Numerical formulation}\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Leaver's method and the Frobenius recurrence}\label{sec:num:leaver}

Factoring the boundary behaviour of Section~\ref{sec:setup:singular} out of
\eqref{eq:radial},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaveransatz}
  R(z) = (z-z_+)^{-\ii\sigma_+}\,(z-z_-)^{\xi}\,e^{\ii\Omega r}\,r^{-3/2}
  \sum_{n\ge0} a_n\,\left(\frac{z-z_+}{z-z_-}\right)^{\!n},
\end{equation}
and clearing denominators produces a linear recurrence with polynomial
coefficients \citep{Leaver1985D51,LeaverD52},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
  \sum_{j=0}^{p} c_j(n)\,a_{n-j} = 0 .
\end{equation}
For generic two-spin parameters the width is $p+1=13$; in the static case
$a=b=0$ it collapses to $9$. The quasinormal condition is the requirement that
the minimal solution of \eqref{eq:recurrence} be selected, which after Gaussian
reduction of \eqref{eq:recurrence} to the three-term form
$\alpha_n a_{n+1}+\beta_n a_n +\gamma_n a_{n-1}=0$ is expressed as the vanishing
of a continued fraction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cf}
  \beta_N - \frac{\alpha_{N-1}\gamma_N}{\beta_{N-1}-}
  \frac{\alpha_{N-2}\gamma_{N-1}}{\beta_{N-2}-}\cdots
  = \frac{\alpha_N\gamma_{N+1}}{\beta_{N+1}-}
    \frac{\alpha_{N+1}\gamma_{N+2}}{\beta_{N+2}-}\cdots ,
\end{equation}
whose $N$-th inversion is best conditioned for the $N$-th overtone. We stress
that the inversion index changes the conditioning of \eqref{eq:cf} but not its
zero set; it does not by itself select an overtone, and treating it as though it
did produces duplicated branches.

\subsection{Large-$n$ asymptotics of the recurrence}\label{sec:num:tail}

The convergence rate of \eqref{eq:cf} is governed by the large-$n$ behaviour of
the ratio $R_n=a_n/a_{n-1}$. Writing the characteristic equation of
\eqref{eq:recurrence} as $A(1/R)=0$, one finds that $x=1$ is a \emph{multiple}
root, with multiplicity at least five in $A$ and exactly four in the neighbouring
coefficient polynomials. The usual integer-power ansatz therefore degenerates,
and half-integer powers survive:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
  R_n = 1 + u_1 n^{-1/2} + u_2 n^{-1} + \Ord(n^{-3/2}),
  \qquad
  u_1 = -\sqrt{-2c},\qquad c = \ii\Omega\,(r_+-r_-) .
\end{equation}
The sign of $u_1$ is fixed by minimality. Including the leading term of
\eqref{eq:tail} in the tail of \eqref{eq:cf} reduces the depth required for a
given accuracy by a factor $\approx1.35$, equivalent to $1.4$ additional digits
at depth $200$. Higher coefficients are not available to us: the Gaussian
reduction degrades beyond $n\sim500$--$800$, where the backward ratio recursion
collapses onto a spurious, problem-independent $R_n-1=2/n$. The derivation of
\eqref{eq:tail} is given in Appendix~\ref{app:frobenius}.

\subsection{Exterior complex scaling}\label{sec:num:scaling}

An entirely independent formulation is obtained by rotating the radial contour
into the complex plane beyond a matching radius,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contour}
  r = r_m + \varsigma\,e^{\ii\vartheta},\qquad \varsigma\in[0,L],
\end{equation}
and discretizing \eqref{eq:radial} by Chebyshev collocation on $\varsigma$. The
outgoing solution \eqref{eq:infinityBC} decays along \eqref{eq:contour} provided
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anglecondition}
  \tan\vartheta > -\frac{\Imm\,\Omega}{\Rem\,\Omega} ,
\end{equation}
which defines a minimum admissible angle $\vartheta_{\min}$. Under this
transformation a genuine resonance becomes an eigenvalue of the scaled operator
and is independent of $\vartheta$, whereas the rotated continuum sweeps with
$\vartheta$; the construction thus discriminates resonances from artifacts
without recourse to any root finder. Condition \eqref{eq:anglecondition} plays a
decisive role in Section~\ref{sec:quasiresonant}.

\subsection{Continuation and branch identity}\label{sec:atlas:identity}

Branches are identified by continuation history, never by sorting frequencies.
At an avoided crossing two branches exchange their order in $\Rem\,\omega$ while
remaining distinct modes, so any sort-based labelling silently interchanges
them. Each continuation step is seeded by a quadratic extrapolation of the
preceding points,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:predictor}
  \omega^{\rm pred}_{k+1} = \sum_{j=0}^{2} L_j(\tau_{k+1})\,\omega_{k-j},
\end{equation}
with $L_j$ the Lagrange polynomials in the arclength parameter $\tau$, and the
corrected root is accepted only if the predictor miss
$\varepsilon_{k+1}=|\omega_{k+1}-\omega^{\rm pred}_{k+1}|$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:misstest}
  \varepsilon_{k+1} \;\le\;
  \max\Big(4\,\operatorname{median}\{\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_k\},\
  \ 0.02\max(|\omega_{k+1}|,1)\Big).
\end{equation}
The first argument of \eqref{eq:misstest} is essential. The predictor miss is
the local truncation error of \eqref{eq:predictor} and therefore follows a
smooth trend along a genuine branch, so a jump appears as a sudden multiple of
the running median. A tolerance proportional to $|\omega|$ alone is a
\emph{constant}, and at $|\omega|\approx3.7$ it admitted a jump in which
$\Imm\,\omega$ reversed sign in its trend; the contaminated point became, until
it was detected, the tightest reported candidate.

\subsection{Coverage}\label{sec:atlas:coverage}

The atlas comprises $50\,558$ points over seven independent sectors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sectors}
  (0,0),\ (1,1),\ (-1,-1),\ (1,0),\ (2,0),\ (2,1),\ (2,2),
\end{equation}

$\ell_{\min}=|m_1|+|m_2|$, and $N=0,\dots,3$, over

frequencies.

\subsection{The bounds}\label{sec:results:bounds}

At every parameter point we evaluate the minimum pairwise gap between distinct
branches of the same sector; at the tightest candidates and at a deterministic
random subsample we evaluate in addition the invariant estimator \eqref{eq:sep}.
The two tiers fail in different directions --- the gap sees only branches that
were computed, whereas \eqref{eq:sep} responds to any nearby zero --- and the
use of both is what makes the conclusion trustworthy, as
Section~\ref{sec:results:collapse} illustrates. Over the domain
\eqref{eq:domain} we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
  \min_{\rm atlas}|\omega_i-\omega_j| = 0.5116, \qquad
  \min\left|\frac{a_1}{a_2}\right| = 0.3107, \qquad
  \min|D| = 0.2618, \qquad
  \kappa_{\max} = 8.73\times10^{3},
\end{equation}
the last against $\kappa=18.3$ at an ordinary control mode. An EP2 requires the
first three quantities to vanish simultaneously and $\kappa$ to diverge. None
approaches zero: the minimum gap is $\approx0.51$ in a region where
$|\omega|\approx2$--$4$, a relative separation of order $15\%$.
Figure~\ref{fig:exclusion} displays the gap landscape and the distributions
underlying \eqref{eq:bounds}. A summary is collected in Table~\ref{tab:bounds}.

\subsection{Verification of the tightest candidates}\label{sec:results:verify}

The ten tightest candidates were subjected to the full gate of
Table~\ref{tab:gate}. All ten are rejected, and all ten exhibit the same
signature: the argument principle \eqref{eq:argprinciple} finds two zeros and
both are located without a starting guess, but there is no coalescence, no
monodromy interchange around a parameter loop, and no square-root splitting.
Since G2 and G3 are preconditions --- they establish only that two distinct
roots lie near one another --- the correct classification is \emph{avoided
crossing} rather than \emph{unresolved}. This is the behaviour familiar from
eigenvalue repulsion in the Kerr--Newman family
\citep{Diasetal2021B19,Davey2023B21}, here occurring without the accompanying
exceptional points.

\subsection{Branch-label collapse, and the necessity of two tiers}
\label{sec:results:collapse}

Four point-pairs whose gaps lay at the level of machine precision were
identified as \emph{branch-label collapses}: continuation had lost one of two
labelled branches, so that both labels designated the same mode. Their invariant
separation \eqref{eq:sep} exceeds the apparent gap by eleven orders of
magnitude, and the argument principle finds exactly one zero inside a contour
that the gap alone would have suggested contained two. These points are excluded
and counted in Table~\ref{tab:census}.

This is not a technicality. In an earlier construction of the atlas the same
defect affected two hundred parameter points, and the tier-one minimum read
$4.3\times10^{-15}$ --- a false positive that a single-diagnostic search would
have reported as a coalescence. It was detected only because the two tiers
disagreed in a manner no genuine exceptional point can produce: a true
coalescence drives \emph{both} quantities to zero, whereas a lost label makes the
gap vanish while leaving the separation of order unity.

\subsection{The effective two-branch model}\label{sec:results:effective}

Using the invariants \eqref{eq:TD}, we fit $D$ as a polynomial in
$(s,t=\delta^2,\mu)$ and score the fit on held-out points. The result is a
relative out-of-sample error of $1.7\times10^{-5}$ in the diagonal sector
$(1,1)$, against $1.4\times10^{-1}$ in the off-diagonal sector $(2,0)$. The
four-order-of-magnitude discrepancy is an independent confirmation of
Table~\ref{tab:stabilizers}: only diagonal sectors are analytic in $\delta^2$,
so only there is a fit in $t$ the correct model. The effective description thus
inherits the symmetry classification rather than assuming it.

\subsection{Conditioning}\label{sec:results:pseudo}

Quasinormal spectra of black holes are notoriously sensitive to perturbations of
the operator, and the appropriate diagnostic is the pseudospectrum, whose
interpretation depends on the norm chosen
\citep{Nollert1996C33,Jaramillo2020C36,Jaramillo2021C37,Destounisetal2021C38,%
Cheungetal2021C39,Bertietal2022C41,Destounis2023C42,Yangetal2024C43,%
Shenetal2025C44,TrefethenE72,GasperinC40}. We therefore state ours explicitly.
The operator is the exterior-complex-scaled collocation matrix of
Section~\ref{sec:num:scaling}, with $\Lambda(\omega)$ recomputed at every
$\omega$; the norm is the matrix $2$-norm on collocation coefficients; and the
eigenvalue condition number at a simple root is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
  \kappa = \frac{\|u\|\,\|v\|}{\big|v^{\dagger}\,T'(\omega_*)\,u\big|},
\end{equation}
with $u$ and $v$ the right and left null vectors. This is a
\emph{discretization} norm rather than a continuum energy norm, and we make no
continuum pseudospectral claim. At the strongest interaction
$\kappa=8.73\times10^{3}$ against $18.3$ at an ordinary mode: elevated by a
factor $476$, as one expects at a genuine avoided crossing, but finite.

\subsection{Time-domain response}\label{sec:results:ringdown}

For a $2\times2$ generator the propagator is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:propagator}
  e^{-\ii Ht} = e^{-\ii\bar\omega t}
  \left[\cos(\Delta t)\,\mathbb{1}
  - \ii\,\frac{\sin(\Delta t)}{\Delta}\,\big(H-\bar\omega\mathbb{1}\big)\right],
  \qquad \bar\omega=\tfrac{T}{2},\quad \Delta=\tfrac{\sqrt D}{2},
\end{equation}
which tends to the secular form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:secular}
  e^{-\ii Ht} \longrightarrow e^{-\ii\bar\omega t}
  \left[\mathbb{1} - \ii t\big(H-\bar\omega\mathbb{1}\big)\right],
  \qquad D\to0,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the double-pole response characteristic of an exceptional point
\citep{PanossoMacedoetal2025B30,Nakamoto2026A17}.

A tempting criterion is to compare the beat time
$t_{\rm beat}=2\pi/|\Rem(\omega_+-\omega_-)|$ with the damping time
$\tau=1/|\Imm\,\bar\omega|$ and to declare the pair EP-like whenever
$t_{\rm beat}\gg\tau$, on the grounds that the modes then decay before they can
be resolved. \emph{That criterion is insufficient}, and the present system shows
why. At the strongest interactions $t_{\rm beat}/\tau$ reaches
$1.4\times10^{4}$, and yet nothing EP-like occurs. The reason is visible in the
decomposition of the splitting: these branches separate almost entirely in
$\Imm\,\omega$, by $0.53$, and hardly at all in $\Rem\,\omega$, by between
$8\times10^{-4}$ and $0.07$. They possess nearly equal oscillation frequencies
but very different decay rates, so that one companion simply dies first. Secular
growth as in \eqref{eq:secular} requires coalescence in the \emph{full complex}
frequency, not near-degeneracy of the real parts alone. Consistently, the
template $(A+Bt)e^{-\ii\bar\omega t}$ improves upon a single damped exponential
by only a factor $2.4$ in relative residual while carrying twice as many free
amplitudes. We find no exceptional phenomenology in the ringdown, and note that
the distinction is material for analyses that adopt near-degeneracy of
$\Rem\,\omega$ as a proxy \citep{Motohashi2024B24,Kubota2025B27}.

\subsection{The scaling-angle obstruction}\label{sec:quasi:angle}

The same regime resolves a question about solver behaviour which proves to be
physics rather than numerics. By \eqref{eq:anglecondition} the exterior
complex-scaling contour requires
$\tan\vartheta > -\Imm\,\Omega/\Rem\,\Omega$. As $\Rem\,\Omega\to0$ the outgoing
wave ceases to oscillate and becomes a pure growing exponential, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anglelimit}
  \vartheta_{\min} \longrightarrow 90^\circ
  \qquad\text{as}\qquad \Rem\,\Omega\to0 ,
\end{equation}
and \emph{no admissible contour exists}. Figure~\ref{fig:quasi}(b) shows
$\vartheta_{\min}(\mu)$ for several damping rates. We measure
$\vartheta_{\min}=89.92^\circ$ and $89.81^\circ$ at the two points where the
recurrence-free solvers fail, against $4.4^\circ$ at an ordinary near-extremal
point where they succeed.

Equation \eqref{eq:anglelimit} is a limit of the \emph{formulation}, not a
convergence failure: no increase of resolution, of working precision, or of
domain decomposition can remove it. This is precisely why a multidomain
refinement of the near-horizon region --- which resolves $r_+-r_-$ by
construction --- failed to help.

\subsection{Consequences: the controlling variable is $\mu$, not the spin}
\label{sec:quasi:consequences}

It follows that difficulty in this parameter range is governed by $\mu$ and not
by proximity to extremality. Ordinary modes continue smoothly to
$\mathcal{E}=0.0103$ --- essentially extremal --- with residuals
$\sim10^{-13}$ over thirty-seven consecutive steps. Attributing such failures to
near-extremality, natural though it is given the extensive literature on nearly
extremal spectra \citep{Yangetal2012B18,Cavalcante2024B23}, would misidentify
the mechanism here. The principled tool for this regime is a hyperboloidal
formulation, which regularizes the outgoing behaviour geometrically rather than
by rotation and for which $\Rem\,\Omega\to0$ is not a special case
\citep{Jaramillo2020C36,PanossoMacedoetal2025B30}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
%=========================================================================

We state the limitations without softening them.

\paragraph{Boundary of the search.} The minimum in \eqref{eq:bounds} is attained
on the boundary of the domain \eqref{eq:domain}, at $s=0.42$, $\delta=-0.20$,
$\mu=1.8$, as Fig.~\ref{fig:exclusion}(a) shows. A targeted probe beyond the box
finds the gap continuing to fall to $0.472$ at $s=0.45$, but \emph{rising} again
at $\mu=3.0$ and $\mu=3.6$, to between $0.70$ and $1.12$; the trend therefore
does not extrapolate to zero. Nevertheless no claim is made for $s>0.45$ or
$\mu>1.8$. Overtones $N>3$ and higher $\ell$ were not searched, and the
behaviour of highly damped overtones is known to be delicate
\citep{Nollert1996C33,Miyachi2025C45}.

\paragraph{The quasiresonant regime.} No search for exceptional points was
performed there. By Section~\ref{sec:quasi:angle} the recurrence-free solvers are
inapplicable by formulation, so no independent confirmation is available and the
evidentiary standard of Table~\ref{tab:gate} cannot be met. This is the one
region of the studied sectors in which an exceptional point could still be
hiding.

\paragraph{Certification.} Certification was attempted and is deliberately
limited in scope. Using interval and ball arithmetic
\citep{MooreE73,TuckerE74,Breiding2020E69,Duff2024E70} we obtain a Krawczyk
existence and uniqueness certificate for finite-dimensional systems, and an Arb
enclosure of the angular eigenvalue of the truncated Jacobi matrix, validated by
recovering the exact value $\ell(\ell+2)$ at $c^2=0$. The \emph{radial} problem
is not certified: its recurrence coefficients are obtained by a discrete Fourier
transform for which we possess no rigorous aliasing bound, so the truncation
contribution to the error budget
\begin{equation}\label{eq:errorbudget}
  E_{\rm total} \le E_{\rm discretization} + E_{\rm truncation}
  + E_{\rm arithmetic} + E_{\rm root}
\end{equation}
cannot be bounded. We therefore claim no truncated-recurrence certificate
anywhere, and no continuum certificate at all. Details are in
Appendix~\ref{app:certification}.

\paragraph{Status of the two results.} The numerical result is a bounded
exclusion over a declared domain. It is not a theorem, and it must not be
conflated with Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}, which is exact and holds everywhere.

\section{Discussion and outlook}\label{sec:discussion}
%=========================================================================

Exceptional lines \emph{do} occur in the de Sitter members of this family, in
the plane spanned by the scalar mass and the spin, and in the near-Nariai limit
\citep{Nakamoto2026A17}. The present $\Lambda=0$ result is a contrast between
spacetimes rather than a contradiction: the de Sitter mechanism relies upon the
cosmological horizon and the P\"oschl--Teller reduction, neither of which has an
asymptotically flat analogue, and the eigenvalue repulsions accompanying it
\citep{Daveyetal2022A13} are likewise a de Sitter phenomenon. Nothing here is
evidence about those results.

Within the asymptotically flat, doubly rotating case, the structure we find is
sharply organized. The most natural route to exceptional behaviour is closed
exactly, by the very symmetry that generates the candidate degeneracies. The
surviving route --- collisions between $(\ell,N)$ branches inside a fixed sector
--- is numerically weak: those branches repel with a gap that never falls below
half a unit, and they interact predominantly through their damping rates rather
than their frequencies, so that even the strongest interaction leaves no imprint
upon the ringdown.

It is worth emphasising that this is a result about a parameter space of
\emph{exactly} the dimension at which exceptional sets should be generic. The
doubly rotating background supplies two spin parameters and a mass, and
codimension-two sets are curves in three parameters
\citep{Bergholtz2019E63}; their absence is therefore informative rather than
expected.

Two methodological points may outlive the specific application. First, the
derivative of a spectral condition is not an invariant
(Proposition~\ref{prop:noninvariance}) and cannot bound the distance to an
exceptional point; searches which use it should be re-examined. Second, a small
computed gap between two \emph{labels} is evidence about the labels before it is
evidence about the physics, and a search employing a single diagnostic cannot
distinguish the two.

The natural next step is a hyperboloidal formulation, the only method that
remains applicable as $\Rem\,\Omega\to0$, and therefore the only route into the
quasiresonant regime --- the sole region of the studied sectors which the
present work could not cover.

\section{Separation of the massive Klein--Gordon equation}\label{app:sep}
%=========================================================================

We record the route by which \eqref{eq:angular}--\eqref{eq:WG} were obtained,
since the derivation was performed symbolically rather than transcribed.

With the ansatz \eqref{eq:modeansatz}, the equation
$\partial_\nu(\sqrt{|g|}\,g^{\nu\rho}\partial_\rho\Phi)
= \mu^2\sqrt{|g|}\,\Phi$ requires the inverse metric. Rather than inverting the
full $5\times5$ matrix, note that \eqref{eq:metric} is block diagonal: the pair
$(r,\theta)$ is diagonal, and the remaining $(t,\phi,\psi)$ block is
$3\times3$. Inverting the small block and using
$\sqrt{|g|}=r\Sigma\sin\theta\cos\theta$ yields the wave operator in closed
form.

Substituting $u=\cos^2\!\theta$ converts the resulting expression into a
rational function of $(r,u)$, at which point exact cancellation becomes
tractable. Writing the separation quotient as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sepquotient}
  Q(r,u) = \frac{1}{R}\,\mathcal{L}_r R + \frac{1}{S}\,\mathcal{L}_u S ,
\end{equation}
separability is the statement $\partial^2 Q/\partial r\,\partial u\equiv0$, which
we verify in exact rational arithmetic. Partial fractions in $u$ then isolate
the angular operator and read off \eqref{eq:angular}; the remainder is
\eqref{eq:radial}. Two checks are applied: $\sqrt{|g|}=r\Sigma\sin\theta\cos\theta$
must hold identically, and at $a=b=\mu=0$, $m_1=m_2=0$ the radial bracket must
reduce to $\omega^2r^4/(r^2-M)-\Lambda$ with $\Lambda=\ell(\ell+2)$, the standard
five-dimensional Schwarzschild--Tangherlini scalar equation.

\section{Frobenius exponents and the asymptotic tail}\label{app:frobenius}
%=========================================================================

\subsection*{B.1\quad Exponents at the horizons}

Near $z=z_+$ the radial equation \eqref{eq:radial} has a regular singular point.
Writing $R\sim(z-z_+)^{\varrho}$ and retaining the leading behaviour of $W^2/(r^4\Delta)$
gives the indicial equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indicial}
  \varrho^2 + \sigma_+^2 = 0 ,
  \qquad \varrho = \pm\ii\sigma_+ ,
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_+$ as in \eqref{eq:sigmaplus}. The factorization
$W(z_+) = (z_++a^2)(z_++b^2)\,\big(\omega-m_1\Omega_a-m_2\Omega_b\big)$, which
follows from \eqref{eq:WG} and \eqref{eq:horizondata}, is what makes
\eqref{eq:sigmaplus} take the stated form. The quasinormal condition selects
$\varrho=-\ii\sigma_+$, i.e.\ purely ingoing at the horizon.

\subsection*{B.2\quad Behaviour at infinity}

At large $r$, \eqref{eq:radial} becomes
$R'' + \tfrac{3}{r}R' + \big(\Omega^2 + \Ord(r^{-2})\big)R = 0$, with $\Omega$
as in \eqref{eq:Omegadef}. Because the potential is even in $r$ there is no
$\Ord(1/r)$ term, so the WKB exponent contains no logarithm and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymptotic}
  R \sim e^{\pm\ii\Omega r}\,r^{-3/2}\Big(1+\Ord(r^{-1})\Big),
\end{equation}
the outgoing branch being selected. The absence of a Coulomb phase distinguishes
this problem from four-dimensional massive Kerr.

\subsection*{B.3\quad Large-$n$ behaviour of the recurrence}

Set $R_n = a_n/a_{n-1}$ in \eqref{eq:recurrence}. Substituting the ansatz
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailansatz}
  a_n \sim \exp\!\Big(\lambda\sqrt n + \eta\log n
  + d_1 n^{-1/2} + d_2 n^{-1} + \cdots\Big)
\end{equation}
and expanding $a_{n-j}/a_n$ shows that the characteristic polynomial $A(1/R)$
possesses $x=1$ as a root of multiplicity at least five, while the neighbouring
coefficient polynomials have multiplicity exactly four. The balance at order
$n^{-3/2}$ therefore reads $-u_1 A'(1)=0$ with $A'(1)=0$, so the integer-power
series degenerates and half-integer powers survive, giving \eqref{eq:tail}. Two
independent numerical confirmations are available: the scaling test, in which
$(R_n-1)\sqrt n$ converges while $(R_n-1)\,n$ diverges, and the depth signature
$\exp(-k\sqrt N)$ of the truncation error.

\section{Certification scope}\label{app:certification}
%=========================================================================

Certification claims must name the object certified. We use four labels, in
increasing strength: \emph{arithmetic enclosed} (a ball enclosing the value of a
given finite expression); \emph{algebraic-system certified} (a Krawczyk
certificate for a finite-dimensional nonlinear system); \emph{truncated-recurrence
certified} (the preceding, with the truncation error bounded); and
\emph{continuum certified} (a statement about the differential operator itself).
The error budget \eqref{eq:errorbudget} must be exhibited for any claim.

Given a box $X = \bm x_c \pm \bm\rho$ and $Y\approx J(\bm x_c)^{-1}$, the
Krawczyk operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:krawczyk}
  K(X) = \bm x_c - Y f(\bm x_c) + \big(\mathbb{1} - Y\,J(X)\big)(X-\bm x_c) ,
\end{equation}
where $J(X)$ is an \emph{interval} enclosure of the Jacobian over $X$. If
$K(X)\subset\operatorname{int}X$ then $X$ contains exactly one root; if
$K(X)\cap X=\varnothing$ then $X$ contains none; otherwise the test is
inconclusive. Supplying only the midpoint Jacobian in place of $J(X)$ reduces
\eqref{eq:krawczyk} to a heuristic, and our implementation rejects that input
rather than returning a certificate that is not one.

What is achieved here: the Krawczyk test, validated in both directions
(certifying a unique root, excluding a root-free box, and returning
\emph{inconclusive} rather than guessing on an over-large box); and an Arb ball
enclosure of the angular eigenvalue of the $N$-truncated Jacobi matrix,
validated by recovering the exact value $\ell(\ell+2)$ at $c^2=0$.

What is not achieved, and why: the coefficients of \eqref{eq:recurrence} are
extracted by a discrete Fourier transform on a circle, and we have no rigorous
bound on its aliasing error, so $E_{\rm truncation}$ in \eqref{eq:errorbudget}
cannot be bounded. Closing this gap requires either such a bound or an exact
symbolic route to the coefficients which ball arithmetic can enclose end to end.

\section*{Data availability}
%=========================================================================

All code, machine-readable results and reproduction scripts are available at the
project repository. The central result is regenerated by a single command from a
clean clone; the branch atlas is regenerated from a deterministic driver and
verified against a per-shard SHA-256 manifest rather than distributed. Every
figure in this paper is generated from the same JSON records that the text
quotes, so that no figure can drift from the result it illustrates.
